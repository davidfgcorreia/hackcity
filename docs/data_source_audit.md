# Data-source audit for analytics and prediction

**Checked 24 September 2026.** This inventory records what is in `datasets/`, including the later `finalset/` delivery, what the supplied GBFS directory actually returns, and additional public sources checked for a Cascais planning demo. A successful one-time request does not establish historical availability or production reliability. Keep downloaded or derived data outside the supplied originals and record retrieval time, license, version and checksum for every later ingest.

## Supplied files: what can be measured

The complete new-delivery inventory, extraction checks, join tests and usage limits are in [finalset_data_review.md](finalset_data_review.md). `finalset` now contains **two distinct transit periods**: the 2026 multioperator validations/vehicle positions that overlap the Cascais bicycle sample, and a joined October 2025 APEX/ride/vehicle-event/plan bundle. The 2026 validations change the earlier data gap: transit boardings and repeatable **card** histories are present, though bicycle trips still have no user identifier. Card hash is not verified person identity, boarding is not alighting, and cross-operator card sequences do not by themselves prove a complete journey. The 2026 validation metadata restricts use and publication beyond the hackathon; keep identifiers out of maps and exports. See the [finalset review](finalset_data_review.md) for the exact field and join checks.

The bicycle Excel files each contain an absent penultimate worksheet row. Their counts here and in [initial.md](initial.md) use nonempty physical records after the header. `datasets/finalset` is currently ignored by Git, so collaborators need the source archives delivered separately.

## Realistic first demo with the supplied data

1. Map valid bicycle starts/ends, existing station polygons, short-period outside-station candidates, current status snapshot, municipal bus and rail stops/routes, scheduled frequency, and separate transport-gap measures.
2. Show **three separate experimental next-day forecast layers**, as requested by the project team: bicycle starts/ends by existing station or area, possible abandonment hotspots, and a bus-to-bike demand proxy by stop. Use chronological held-out slices, simple baselines, error summaries and low-sample warnings. The exact label and spatial unit for each layer still need approval. Outside-station trip ends are only candidates; they cannot be silently promoted to verified abandonments.
3. Add observed MobiCascais **boarding counts** by stop and hour after matching its 2026 stop IDs to the dated plan. Keep bus/rail-to-bike opportunity as a **space-and-time proximity proxy** around stops, never as a verified transfer to a bicycle; no card key exists in the bicycle trips. Study cross-operator card sequences separately and only in aggregate. Present scheduled next transit stop, nearby bicycle station and bicycle destination area as separate targets; MobiCascais alighting is not observed.
4. Compare bicycle-station, bus, rail and walking-access fixes for transport gaps. Display observed evidence, forecasts and scenarios in separate layers.
5. Show Cascais historical bus-delay hotspots as context for bicycle-station planning. The required Cascais road-jam layer waits for a verified local feed; the supplied Lisbon-only Waze rows cannot populate it. Report Cascais historical departure delay by route, arrival delay at stops and corridor travel time separately, starting with continuous delay minutes rather than a late threshold. Compare weekday/weekend, local 07:00–10:00 versus 16:00–19:00 and each route by hour, with sample counts. Use OpenStreetMap paths checked against municipal map layers to show routes and stops within a five-minute walk beside a candidate bicycle station, with median and 90th-percentile nonnegative delay minutes from all observed bus service trips when at least 75% of all scheduled services in the selected stop, route and time slice have reliable observed matches and at least 10 matched services span three distinct days within every displayed stop-route-time slice; report earliness separately. Show observed and scheduled corridor times side by side; if no reliable schedule match exists, label observed vehicle travel time without calling it delay. Defer both the road-jam map and road forecasting until a suitable Cascais road feed is verified, and do not forecast bus delays. A bus-delay hotspot is a service-performance measure, not a direct road-jam observation. Validate the bus schedule–vehicle join and define delay before showing each historical measure.

The supplied card hashes support bounded transit validation sequences, but they cannot establish a named person's complete daily itinerary, an actual bus-to-bicycle transfer, a first-year effect of a new station, a seasonally validated annual demand model, or a Cascais road-jam map or forecast from the Lisbon-only Waze extract. No public Cascais road-jam time series was verified in this audit. [Waze for Cities](https://support.google.com/waze/partners/answer/10618035?hl=en) documents a localized partner feed available through Partner Hub, but it requires partner access and no Cascais feed was found publicly accessible. [Google's public-sector FAQ](https://cities.google/intl/pt-BR_ALL/faqs) says Waze for Cities usually has no financial cost for partners and can expose years of historical traffic data; Cascais Próxima or the municipality must verify eligibility, access, Cascais polygon coverage and data-use terms before using it. The municipal open-data portal lists static transport geography and road-intervention layers, not a verified historic jam or speed series. The attached bus positions may support a **bus travel-speed or delay proxy** after route matching, but that is a different measure from road congestion. These remain separate goals with explicit data gates in [analytics_prediction_requirements.md](analytics_prediction_requirements.md).

## Weather-impact analysis that the current sample can support

Join hourly historical weather to local event time in `Europe/Lisbon`, retaining UTC and the weather grid point. For bicycles, compare valid departures/returns and candidate outside-station cases on rainy versus dry hours, accounting for weekday, hour, school calendar and geography. For buses, compare available punctuality and service-time measures by line and hour. Report raw differences and adjusted associations with uncertainty; the short extract may contain too few weather events for a stable estimate. The nested CP vehicle positions offer a possible rail observation source, but observed train timing needs a validated join to CP GTFS before rail-weather analysis. No walking counts are attached, so observed walking-choice effects cannot be measured. A later journey score can show a weather scenario for walk/cycle time or comfort, but its adjustment must be calibrated rather than guessed.
