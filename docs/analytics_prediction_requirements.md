# Analytics and prediction requirements: recommended specification and decision log

**Recommended specification · 24 September 2026 · Project-team choices plus explicit assumptions**

This document records the separate analytics review requested in [initial.md](initial.md), §§4–6. It covers municipal recovery oversight, company service and station balancing, new station opportunities, multimodal journeys, and a proposed congestion hotspot demonstration. The **recommended defaults** below settle the remaining design choices for a bounded first demo; historical interview notes remain for provenance. No ranking or forecast is a municipality-approved operational recommendation yet. The existing operational requirements remain in [operations_requirements.md](operations_requirements.md).

## Decision gate

Review **one feature at a time**, in the order selected by the project team. Do not treat the next feature's proposed behavior as agreed until the current feature has a clear:

1. **Purpose:** decision it changes, decision owner, users, cadence, geographic and time scope.
2. **Output:** map, table, alert or export; units, filters, granularity, and distinction between observed, inferred, forecast and scenario values.
3. **Method:** authoritative inputs, join keys, time zones, spatial rules, definitions, model or calculation, update schedule, and fallback for missing data.
4. **Proof:** historical baseline, validation method, acceptance target, known limitations, and person who approves the result.

The requested **95% confidence** is a requirements-understanding gate, not a statistical score or a claim that a prediction is 95% accurate. The team has ended the interview and authorized recommended defaults for remaining choices. Mark a bounded demo `ready to specify` when its purpose, output, method and proof are concrete and internally consistent; mark unverified inputs or owner approval separately. A model's uncertainty and forecast accuracy must be measured separately.

## Recommended defaults for remaining decisions

These choices complete the **first, one-off internal Cascais planning demo** without treating unobserved behavior as fact. They are implementation assumptions proposed by the project team, not evidence of approval from Cascais Próxima. Record them in the map legend and metric dictionary. The operations lead signs off the ranking and operational metrics before they are described as approved recommendations.

### Common display and data rules

- Show source, observed date range, retrieval date, geographic coverage, sample size and data-quality status with every layer. Separate **observed**, **inferred proxy**, **experimental forecast** and **what-if scenario** views. Use `Europe/Lisbon` for local grouping while retaining original timestamps and UTC conversions.
- Use a 250-metre square grid in EPSG:3763 (Portugal mainland metric coordinates) for exploratory bicycle endpoint and parking-candidate hotspot maps; retain the original point and station-polygon geometry for calculations. Aggregate repeated records from one parking interval before counting a case. Suppress an unstable grid score when fewer than five distinct intervals contribute, but display the count and an “insufficient observations” note. The 250-metre grid is a display and screening unit, not a legal station boundary or a 30-metre parking rule.
- The first demo is an internal map plus a ranked candidate table and metric details, exportable as CSV and PDF in Portuguese. Provide date, weekday/weekend, hour, route, station and area filters. A candidate detail panel shows each score component, underlying values, support counts, confidence/uncertainty labels, the applicable transit routes and stops, and the planning assumptions. No public person-level map is part of this demo.
- Treat missing required data as **unavailable**, never as zero. Show a source-coverage note when a metric is unavailable. Use a later chronological slice for any illustrative forecast evaluation. A forecast must beat the relevant simple baseline before it is described as useful; otherwise retain it as an experiment with its measured error.

### Recovery, contract and station-service metrics

- Count candidate parking cases by distinct bicycle parking interval. Apply the existing operational rule only when the bicycle is outside the station polygon plus 30 metres and remains there **strictly more than 120 minutes** with supporting temporal evidence. Report candidate, supported and verified cases separately. The case cohort for recovery rates is eligible cases whose trigger falls within the selected period; open cases whose response deadline is beyond the data cutoff are censored rather than counted as failures.
- Report company recovery share, response-time median and 90th percentile, municipal pickup share and time, failed-visit rate, pickups per crew-hour and kilometres per pickup. Use provider movement events only when actor attribution is reliable; otherwise mark the recovery actor unknown. Cost per pickup is an **estimate** until labor, vehicle, fuel and handling rates are supplied. Penalties require authoritative municipal finance records and remain outside the first demo.
- Show valid bicycle starts, ends and net flow by station, weekday and hour. Assign a trip to an existing station only by its versioned `station_area` polygon; the 30-metre buffer is for parking eligibility, not trip-to-station assignment. Poll the public GBFS `station_status` feed prospectively for actual availability history. Do not derive absolute stock, stock-out duration or capacity from trip flow alone. Define a measured stock-out as zero available rentable bicycles during published service hours once reliable status history exists; report data gaps separately.
- Keep recovery workload prediction out of the first demo because verified municipal outcomes and a real-time stable bicycle identifier are absent. If those arrive later, test next-day eligible-case counts by area against a same-weekday baseline before considering operational use; never dispatch automatically from a forecast.

### Bicycle station screening and demand forecasts

- Generate **candidate areas**, not approved installation sites, from valid trip starts/ends, supported parking intervals, transit access, population/destinations and gaps in walking/cycling coverage. Use the 250-metre grid for screening, then place a candidate point only after safe access, land permission, accessibility, capacity, nearby station displacement and cost have been checked. Unchecked legal or site feasibility is a visible blocker to an installation recommendation.
- Propose a 0–1 candidate score with visible weights: observed bicycle demand **35%**, supported outside-station parking opportunity **25%**, complete-journey improvement **20%**, transport-coverage gap **10%**, population/access-equity context **5%**, and the agreed bus-delay component **5%**. Normalize each measured component from a stated comparison set and show raw values and ranks with and without each component. These first five weights fill the previously unallocated 95% as **provisional assumptions**; the Cascais Próxima operations lead must approve them. Treat legal/safety feasibility as a gate, not a weighted benefit.
- For the bus-delay component, pool reliable matched bus services at stops within the agreed five-minute path walk and take the pooled median delay `d` minutes. Use `min(1, max(0, d / 10))`; no delay is 0 and 10 or more minutes is 1. If no reliable nearby delay exists, use **0.5**, label it missing, and show a sensitivity rank with the bus component removed. The small positive boost for delayed buses is a hypothesis about cycling opportunity, not a measured mode shift.
- Report starts, ends and **proxy** bus/rail-connected bicycle demand as three distinct candidate forecast targets for the first month after opening. Use comparable existing station catchments matched by observed trip density, population and transit service, with a simple range across comparables. Subtract likely use displaced from nearby existing stations when estimating **incremental** municipal demand. A new site has no direct training label; do not claim a calibrated first-month forecast from the current three-week sample. Show typical annual demand and first-year-after-opening impact as separate future model outputs only after at least a full seasonal history and later-station validation exist.
- For the current short sample, the three agreed next-day experimental layers use existing-station or 250-metre-cell trip starts/ends, possible outside-station parking intervals by cell, and a bus-to-bike proximity proxy by stop. Train on earlier dates and score only later dates against a same-weekday/hour historical-rate baseline and a previous-day baseline where feasible. Report MAE for counts, precision/recall for hotspot flags, counts of cells with insufficient history and prediction intervals from time-split residuals. If the short sample cannot support a valid holdout, show descriptive history only for that target.

- Compare complete walk, bicycle, bus and rail paths using network access plus scheduled waiting, transfers and final walking. Propose a visible journey-efficiency score: travel time **30%**, waiting **20%**, transfers **15%**, reliability **15%**, accessibility **20%**, after normalizing each to an agreed origin–destination comparison set. Treat an inaccessible path as infeasible for the relevant profile regardless of its score; show fare and cycling safety separately until reliable comparable inputs exist. These weights require municipal approval and sensitivity views; they are not measured travel preferences.
- Report transport problems separately: poor stop access (more than a ten-minute path walk), low service frequency (fewer than two scheduled departures per hour in a chosen service period), weak transfer (scheduled connection requiring more than 15 minutes' wait), and a potential bicycle-station gap where a safe bike link could improve access. These are screening flags, not evidence that a bike station is the best fix. Show bicycle, bus, rail and walking-access alternatives with the evidence and unavailable inputs for each.
- Use historical ERA5 hourly rain, wind and temperature to describe associations with valid bicycle demand and bus punctuality/travel time during the attached sample, stratified by weekday, hour and area/route. Report counts and intervals and avoid causal claims. Keep weather out of the station rank in the first demo; introduce weather-adjusted journey or demand scoring only after longer history and validation. Rail weather reliability requires a validated CP schedule join.

### Historical bus context and future Cascais road data

- M01–M36 route departure delay is computed directly from valid planned and actual departure timestamps in that spreadsheet. For stop arrival and corridor measures, join vehicle run, operational date, stop sequence and the dated GTFS schedule; M37–M44 may show observed times if no schedule match succeeds. Deduplicate repeated vehicle positions, discard invalid coordinates and report missing vehicle coverage. - The 75% reliable-match gate uses **all scheduled services** in the selected stop-route-time slice as denominator; flagged ambiguous closest-time matches are displayed but excluded from its numerator. Require at least ten unambiguous matched services on at least three distinct days in that same slice before showing delay. Otherwise show the stop and a data-quality note with no delay value. Show observed vehicle travel time only where it is supportable, with no “delay” label when the schedule join fails.
