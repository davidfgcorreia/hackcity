## Join and data-quality findings

- The 2026 validation files have nonempty `card_serial_number_hash`, `stop_id`, `line_id` and `vehicle_id` in every inspected row; `trip_id` is blank in **7,216,733** of 10,929,425 rows, mainly outside MobiCascais. MobiCascais has `trip_id` in all **171,578** rows; event types are **168,322 type 1** and **3,256 type 13**, both defined as entry validations in the supplied metadata. There are no MobiCascais exit validations in this week. Therefore a validation measures a boarding, not a known alighting, occupancy, complete itinerary or bus-to-bike transfer.
- **442,515** distinct 2026 card hashes occur at more than one operator during the week. This permits study of *possible* cross-operator card sequences after ordering events and validating times. A card may be used by more than one person, and a later validation does not identify the preceding alighting stop. Aggregate or suppress low-count sequences before any planner-facing display.
- All **171,578** MobiCascais validation stop IDs fail a direct join to `extracted/stops.csv`, despite its 421 Cascais-labeled stops. This is an identifier-system mismatch, not evidence that boarding locations are absent. Obtain the MobiCascais plan valid 31 Aug–6 Sep 2026, then test `agency_id`/`operational_date`/`stop_id` and service-ID matches. MobiCascais validation `trip_id` and 2026 local vehicle `trip_id` overlap for many services, but a reliable join also needs the operational date and event-time checks.
- The four 2025 plans include **10,632,804** stop-time rows in total. All **127,117** ride `trip_id` values match the relevant plan's trip IDs. In the APEX events, **4,511,495 / 4,515,685** `trip_id` values and **all** `stop_id` values match the relevant plan's tables; inspect the 4,190 unmatched trip records before a service-level measure. Join with operator and service date as well as ID, since trip IDs may recur.
- 2026 geohash vehicle files are an event history; their rows and `trip_id` values are not passenger counts. The new 54 paths expand geographic coverage but do not lengthen the one-week observation period. The 2025 and 2026 datasets must never be combined into a single contemporaneous trend or used with the wrong plan version.

## Implications for the project

1. **Cascais transit use is now observed at boarding level.** Replace the previous “no validation data” premise with MobiCascais boardings by line/time/stop once the 2026 stop IDs are resolved. Use the 2026 hashed card IDs only for bounded, aggregated card-sequence research; do not describe them as confirmed people or publish raw IDs.
2. **Bicycle-connected demand remains a proxy.** No key joins a MobiCascais card to a bicycle trip, and MobiCascais exit events are absent. Compare aggregate boardings, vehicle service and bicycle starts/ends in validated spatial/time catchments. Label any transfer opportunity as inferred and report its matching assumptions.
3. **The 2025 bundle supports a separate service analysis.** The matching four plans, ride observations, APEX validations and vehicle events enable operator-level service and punctuality validation for 13–19 Oct 2025. They do not extend the 2026 Cascais planning window or resolve its schedule joins.
4. **Keep the operational bicycle goal unchanged.** None of these TML records supplies stable live bicycle identity, observed bicycle parking dwell, municipal pickup evidence or the recovery contract.

The supplied 2026 validation metadata limits use to analysis, prototypes and experimentation during HackTheCity2026 and states that use, redistribution or publication after the hackathon is not permitted under that delivery's terms. The 2025 APEX file exposes a linkable card field. Treat both as restricted working inputs; document access and retention decisions before producing shared outputs. The metadata describes the 2026 hashes as anonymous, but repeatable card histories still warrant aggregate display and limited access.
